01 / Executive view

The problem is not a lack of ambition.
It is a gap in execution.

Nike has a credible strategic direction and meaningful growth assets. The harder question is whether the company can convert them into full-price demand, faster inventory turns and durable EBIT - before a two-year revenue trough compounds the pressure.

The central consulting question

How can Nike translate Full Stride Ahead into a measurable commercial execution programme that restores sustainable growth and lifts recurring operating profitability from about 6% to 12%-14% by FY2031?

Core recommendation. Treat product newness, marketplace health, inventory productivity and verified cost conversion as one integrated commercial system - not four separate turnaround projects.

What the case concludes

  • Defend what is working: scale Running, Global Football and Women's Basketball / Training.
  • Rebuild product relevance: reduce shoe-model complexity while lifting new / updated models to ~40% of Sportswear footwear revenue by FY2031.
  • Fix revenue quality: stop using broad discounts to solve aged inventory; manage Direct and Wholesale on net contribution.
  • Release cash: improve inventory turns and DIO to support cash generation and dividend resilience.
  • Make execution auditable: link initiatives, owners, KPIs, finance actuals and corrective actions through a monthly review.

03 / Root-cause synthesis

Four connected causes are reinforcing one another.

The report's issue tree points to a self-reinforcing pattern: weaker product vitality creates excess stock; excess stock drives discounting; discounting weakens brand and margin; and long planning cycles make the company slow to correct.

The commercial failure loop

Analytical synthesis of the source report's issue tree and five-whys analysis; not an independently measured causal model.

Product vitality deficit

Over-reliance on Dunk, Air Force 1 and Jordan Retro classics left Sportswear footwear newness below 10%. Performance Sports growth cannot yet replace the decline.

Promotional trap

Overstock and weaker demand shifted Direct into a clearance channel, lowering average selling prices and normalizing shopper expectations for discounts.

Centralization vs. local speed

A long product cycle, outdated China doors and non-aligned digital distribution made local response slower than regional competitors. Some explanations remain hypotheses pending internal data.

Fixed-cost deleverage

A large SG&A base and committed sports-marketing costs were built for a larger revenue base. Falling sales reduced operating leverage while the business still needed reinvestment.

Root-cause synthesisSource report pp. 28-30

04 / Strategic context

Full Stride Ahead is the right direction.
It needs a stronger execution layer.

Nike's November 17, 2026 Investor Day strategy is built around three pillars:

01 / EPIC

Create athlete demand

Make product distinct again through performance innovation across Running, Training, Global Football, Basketball and Outdoor, while renewing Sportswear.

Strategic markers: cut shoe models by ~50%; lift new / updated model revenue share toward ~40%.

02 / CONNECTED

Deepen local relevance

Focus on 23 priority countries; move to Americas, EMEA and APGC from FY2028; increase local design and market autonomy in Greater China.

Strategic marker: priority sports, Sportswear and 23 countries expected to drive >90% of incremental growth.

03 / FAST

Capture demand faster

Link demand, supply, inventory and finance through integrated planning, AI enablement, nearshoring and automated distribution.

Strategic marker: Pace programme targets $2.5B gross run-rate savings.

External competitive test

Adidas is the report's clearest comparator: FY2025 brand currency-neutral growth of 13%, 51.6% gross margin and +13% Greater China growth. Specialist brands such as On, Hoka, ASICS, Salomon and Lululemon are also winning focused category positions.

The comparison suggests Nike's issues are not explained by market conditions alone. It does not prove that Adidas' operating model can be copied wholesale.

The consulting extension

Keep the strategic pillars. Add commercial guardrails, ownership, cash discipline and auditable proof of impact.

  • Channel decisions based on net EBIT contribution, not DTC mix.
  • Finance approval for realized Pace savings.
  • Working-capital targets alongside margin targets.
  • Monthly decisions with defined escalation triggers.

+380 bps gross margin recovery, +120 bps net Pace OpEx, +140 bps revenue scale leverage.

Why gross savings do not equal EBIT

  • The $2.5B gross Pace run-rate is about 526 bps of FY2031 base-case revenue - but not all of it converts into operating profit.
  • About 130 bps of supply-chain / nearshoring / 3PL savings are assumed to flow through cost of sales.
  • Necessary reinvestment absorbs ~140 bps, while wage and operating inflation absorb ~70 bps.
  • The model therefore depends on full-price realization and mix improvement, not cost reduction alone.
Base-case cash outcome: DIO reaches 89 days, inventory turns improve to 4.10x and illustrative FY2031 free cash flow reaches ~$4.4B.

Four quarterly go / no-go signals

  • Sportswear new-model share passes 20% by FY2028.
  • Full-price sell-through expands ~300 bps YoY while promotions stay below 20%.

Two more proof points

  • Greater China improves to better than -5% currency-neutral after the digital reset.
  • At least 65% of gross Pace reductions converts to EBIT while DIO falls below 97 days.

Model assumptions include a FY2027E revenue decline of ~8%, no repeat of the FY2026 tariff refund, a 22% tax rate after FY2027, ~70%-75% operational capture of gross Pace savings and flat annual dividends of ~$2.4B. See the source report for the full scenario architecture.

08 / Business analysis blueprint

Translate the framework into requirements, rules and acceptance.

The case does not stop at a management recommendation. It specifies what the operating layer needs to measure, how exceptions should be handled and how a business owner can prove the workflow works before rollout.

KPI tree - what to measure

Growth & mix

Currency-neutral revenue; Performance Sports; priority-23 country growth.

Revenue quality

Partner POS sell-through; full-price rate; promotional share; ASP; returns.

Product vitality

New-model revenue share; active shoe models; revenue per SKU.

Inventory

Turns; DIO; aged inventory; hero-style availability; forecast accuracy.

Profitability

Normalized gross margin; adjusted operating margin; net Pace savings.

Cash & brand

Free cash flow and dividend cover, plus brand consideration and full-price member activity.

Operating guardrails

  • Escalate if quarterly revenue is >5% below plan.
  • Escalate if gross margin misses plan by >100 bps.
  • Escalate if DIO drifts by >10 days or a milestone slips >30 days.
  • Correct with a named owner and a 60-day remediation plan.
  • Verify Pace benefits through Finance / GL before reporting them as realized EBIT.
Every priority initiative should have one C-suite sponsor and one VP-level owner. The proposal links 40% of annual bonus weighting to audited commercial KPIs.

BA design scope - what the brief must define

  • Data contracts: ERP / GL actuals, wholesale POS, WMS inventory ageing, PLM model and launch data, and CRM / app demand signals.
  • Business rules: normalized one-off items, channel / country ownership, promotion limits, aged-stock routing and exception thresholds.
  • Workflow: initiative owners, approval rights, monthly review, escalation, corrective action and audit trail.
  • Benefits: baseline, gross-to-net Pace bridge, Finance sign-off and realized EBIT tracking.

UAT examples - how acceptance is proven

  • Reconcile segment revenue to the finance ledger without double-counting channel / geography.
  • Normalize the $986M tariff recovery out of recurring margin.
  • Flag a digital promotional share above 20% and route an exception to the right owner.
  • Detect inventory beyond 180 days and test the Factory Store allocation rule.
  • Reject Pace savings as realized EBIT until Finance / GL validation is complete.

10 / Risk, controls & board decisions

Protect the plan from the risks that could undo it.

The transformation should be managed as a sequence of evidence-based gates. The following risk thresholds and decision asks are recommendations from the independent assessment, not Nike's published commitments.

Risks that can break the plan

  • China: prolonged decline after the digital reset; escalate if currency-neutral decline stays worse than 15% for two quarters.
  • Newness: model share fails to reach 15% by FY2028 or launch sell-through is below 50%.
  • Pace leakage: net savings trail plan by more than 20%, or design / technology talent attrition rises.
  • Cash squeeze: trailing 12-month FCF falls below $1.8B while dividend and restructuring commitments persist.

Board decisions requested

  1. Ratify the 20% digital promotional cap and 50% shoe-model reduction.
  2. Mandate Finance / GL verification for all realized Pace savings.
  3. Approve the FY2028 Converse portfolio gate and defined alternatives.
  4. Authorize the Day-90 launch of the global Monthly Commercial Review.

10 / PM, consultant & BA portfolio lens

This case demonstrates how to move from diagnosis to delivery design.

The strongest portfolio story is the end-to-end chain: frame the problem, isolate the real drivers, choose the few moves that matter, translate them into operating requirements and define how success will be monitored.

Product management

Strategic outcomes, prioritised initiatives, product portfolio choices, newness goals, operating roadmap, KPI ownership and decision gates.

Strategy consulting

Financial normalization, market and competitor assessment, issue tree, root-cause diagnosis, scenario analysis, initiative scoring and board recommendations.

Business analysis

12 objectives, CPMF scope, 16 functional requirements, business rules, 24 KPI definitions, stakeholder / RACI design, 20 UAT cases and acceptance criteria.

How to tell the story in an interview

“I started by normalising Nike's financial baseline, then traced the decline to product vitality, discounting, regional execution and inventory. I translated that diagnosis into a five-pillar programme, built an independent scenario model, and designed the governance layer - KPIs, requirements, owners, UAT and a 90-day activation plan - to make the strategy measurable.”

Useful portfolio proof points

  • Separate reported results from recurring performance.
  • Build a scenario model with explicit assumptions and sensitivity.
  • Prioritise a portfolio rather than list generic recommendations.
  • Define metrics with formulas, owners, source systems and cadence.
  • Convert requirements into user stories, acceptance criteria and UAT.
  • Make governance decision-led: thresholds, escalation and corrective actions.

11 / Methodology, assumptions & source notes

What is fact, what is analysis, and what still needs validation?

Evidence standard used in the source

  • Verified fact: published company filings, earnings releases, transcripts or credible industry reporting.
  • Analytical finding: a transparent calculation or synthesis based on the available record.
  • Research-based hypothesis: a plausible explanation that needs internal validation.
  • Illustrative assumption: a modelling parameter or proposed target, not a published Nike commitment.
  • Data gap: information not publicly disclosed and therefore not estimated.

Key limitations

  • Standalone Jordan, Performance Sports and Sportswear profitability is not disclosed.
  • SKU-level inventory ageing and digital return-rate data are not public.
  • Full competitor financials are not available consistently across private / partial-reporting peers.
  • The complete list of Nike's 23 priority countries is not public in the source.
  • All FY2027E-FY2031E scenarios and initiative scores are illustrative.
